Toxic Comments

Six labels at once, and most comments are none of them. The page shows a probability for each, so a rare label can stay quiet.

6 labelsBERT demoTF-IDF baseline
  1. 01 · Split the labelToxic, severe, obscene, threat, insult, identity hate. A comment can be several at once.
  2. 02 · A readable baselineTF-IDF and logistic regression, so each label has precision, recall, and the words behind them.
  3. 03 · A second fitRandom forest on the same features, to see whether precision or recall moves.
  4. 04 · A demo modelBERT fine-tuned on a subset, quantized, and loaded by the Streamlit page.

Question

  • Is this comment toxic, and which of the six kinds, without collapsing them into one yes or no?

Why this path

  • The common “toxic” label has enough examples to learn. Threat and identity hate do not, so one accuracy number would hide that.
  • The linear model is the comparison you can audit. The page serves the BERT weights so a comment can be scored live.

What held

6labels, scored together
0.66toxic F1, linear baseline
1 pageprobability per label

Reading the result

  • On the linear model, toxic and obscene are the labels with a usable F1. The average across all six sits lower because the rare labels are thin.
  • The forest trades recall for precision and ends near the same toxic F1. Neither model is “the wrong one.” They answer different costs.
  • The demo BERT saw a subset, on purpose, so the page could exist on a laptop. A full fine-tune is the longer run of the same setup.

A real output

comment “you are awful and I hate you” toxic 0.46 severe_toxic 0.18 obscene 0.21 threat 0.19 insult 0.29 identity_hate 0.18

Those are the live BERT probabilities, not a hard yes or no.

How to read the table

  • Macro F1 treats every label as equal. That is the honest summary when threat is rare.
  • LIME, in the notebook, shows which words moved a linear decision.
